# Accuracy

English | [日本語](ja/accuracy.md)

Every exported function is tested against R 4.5.2 (`erf` and `erfc`, which R lacks, against
mpmath). The expected values are generated by R scripts
in [`oracle/`](../oracle) and stored in [`test/fixtures/`](../test/fixtures) (one JSON line per
case), so the test suite runs without R. Test inputs always include typical values, far tails
(p = 1e-300), boundaries (0, 1, df → ∞), NaN and Infinity, negative values and huge parameters.

## How a case passes

1. **Matches R** within the tolerance below, or
2. **matches a high-precision reference** (Rmpfr with 256–1300 bits, or exact rationals with
   gmp) within the same tolerance. R itself is wrong in that case, and the test output counts
   it as "more accurate than R", or
3. for quantile functions, **the backward error is no larger than R's**: the distribution
   function evaluated at rStats.js's answer is as close to p as it is at R's answer. A quantile
   function has a large condition number where the distribution function is flat, so a tiny
   difference in p becomes a large difference in the answer and a relative comparison would be
   meaningless there. For discrete distributions, the answer must be the smallest integer that
   satisfies the definition, which is checked with the distribution function.

Cases where rStats.js deliberately differs from R are excluded in the test file
(`knownDifference`) with a written reason and listed in
[differences-from-r.md](differences-from-r.md). The test output reports, for each fixture, how
many cases matched R, matched the reference, passed by backward error and were excluded.

Details of the comparison:

- When the expected value is subnormal (< 2^-1022), an absolute error of 1e-14 × 2^-1022 is
  allowed.
- Log densities and log probabilities near 0 are measured as |difference| / max(|expected|, 1),
  because values near 0 have no special meaning on the log scale.

### Reference values

- **Distributions:** references are not computed for every case (it would take far too long).
  Only the cases that fail against R are written out and computed, and the results are stored
  in `test/fixtures/<name>.ref.json` (`npm run refresh-references`). Each oracle registers its
  reference function with `write_fixture(..., ref = function(row) ...)`.
  Four `pnbinom` references were computed with mpmath instead (60 digits, `betainc`), because
  the Rmpfr reference is wrong or missing there: `pnbinom(1e8, 2.5, mu = 1e6)` in both tails
  (Rmpfr forms p = size/(size + mu) with 256 bits and then takes 1 − (other tail), which leaves
  noise for a result of 8e-106), `pnbinom(300, 50, 0.3, lower.tail = FALSE)` and
  `pnbinom(30, 3, 1 - 1e-10, log.p = TRUE)`. Rerunning `npm run refresh-references` for
  `nbinom` would bring back the wrong Rmpfr value for the first one.
  The incomplete beta function with both parameters large (`pbeta` and `pbinom` with a and b from
  15 to 1e10, around the region of the uniform asymptotic expansion, both tails and the log scale)
  is also checked against mpmath (`scripts/mpmath/largebeta.py`, `audit_pbeta_large` and
  `audit_pbinom_large`, tolerance 1e-14; largest errors 9.7e-16 and 3.1e-15). mpmath's `betainc`
  takes minutes there, so the references are the continued fraction DLMF 8.17.22 at 50 and 80
  digits (kept only where the two agree to 1e-28), cross-checked against numerical integration.
  With one parameter large and the other small (`pbeta` with a from 15 to 1e10 and b from 0.1 to 40,
  and the mirror image; `pt` with df from 30 to 1e12, around the region of the expansion for large a)
  the references are the positive-term series in the frame of the small parameter at 60 and 90 digits
  (`scripts/mpmath/smallb.py`, `audit_pbeta_smallb` and `audit_pt_smallb`, tolerance 1e-14; largest
  errors 5.4e-15 and 1.2e-15, from the series of the incomplete beta function; the cases that take
  the expansion are within 1.2e-15). The R fixtures of that region (`oracle/largea.R`) have an
  Rmpfr reference for every case.
  Five cases where R is off by about 1.0e-14 and the expansion for large a made rStats.js more
  accurate (within 4e-16) have mpmath references (100 and 200 digits, `betainc`, from the double values
  of the arguments; `scripts/mpmath/mismatch_ref.py`), since R was not at hand to compute Rmpfr ones:
  `pbeta(0.1, 2.5, 1000)` (upper tail, and the log of the lower), `pnbinom(30, 1000, mu = 2, log.p =
  TRUE)` and `pf(3, 10, 100, ncp = 0)` (upper tail, and the log of the lower).
  Non-central distributions use Rmpfr sums of the Poisson mixture series or numerical integrals
  (`oracle/_noncentral.R`); regions where no reference can be computed in practical time
  (non-central t with df = 1000 or 1e5, non-central F with df2 = 1e9 and x > 30) are left out of
  the grid.
- **Hypothesis tests:** the oracle stores a reference for every case in the fixture's
  `reference` field. Only means, variances and correlation coefficients need high precision, so
  this is fast. For the t-test, variances and standard errors are computed with Rmpfr (256 bits)
  while the means are first rounded to double, because both R and rStats.js use the difference
  of the rounded means reported in `estimate`; the p-value and quantiles then come from R's
  `pt`/`qt`. Fisher's r × c test enumerates all tables and sums them exactly with gmp. Kendall's
  exact test counts inversions with Rmpfr (1024 bits).
- **Special functions added in 0.6.1** (`choose`, `lchoose`, `factorial`, `lfactorial`,
  `digamma`, `trigamma`, `psigamma`, the Bessel functions, `dmultinom`, and `gammainc`,
  `erfinv`, `erfcinv` and `perm`, which R lacks and whose R fixtures are computed from
  `pgamma` × `gamma`, pracma's definitions and Rmpfr): besides the R
  fixtures, they are checked against mpmath (`scripts/mpmath/*.py`, 60 to 800 digits,
  `test/fixtures/<name>.mpmath.json`), with the same tolerance of 1e-14 (1e-15 for `erfinv`,
  `erfcinv` and `perm`); that comparison uses
  `rCompatible: false`, since the mpmath values are the mathematical function. Where R is
  inaccurate or fails, the references in `<name>.ref.json` were computed with mpmath (100 to 800
  digits, from the double values of the arguments) rather than Rmpfr. Where terms cancel
  (digamma and psigamma for x < 0, J and Y where they oscillate, I for negative non-integer
  orders) the error is measured relative to the size of the cancelling terms, recorded as `scale`
  in the fixture. `psigamma` for x < 0 is allowed 2e-14: the reflection term is a polynomial of
  degree deriv + 1 in cot(pi x), which multiplies the error of cot by up to 101 (also in the R
  comparison). In the R comparison of `besselJ` and `besselY`, a case whose result is much
  smaller than the cancelling terms passes if it is within 1e-14 of the mpmath value relative to
  `scale` (`knownDifference`); R is closer in relative terms there.
- **log1p-type functions** (`log1pmx`, `lgamma1p`, `log1mexp`, `log1pexp`, which R has only in its C
  API): the fixtures hold correctly rounded values computed with Rmpfr (512 bits, more for
  `lgamma1p` at tiny x so that 1 + x is exact), not R's output, and are compared with a tolerance
  of 1e-14 (the largest observed error is 1.7e-15, for `lgamma1p`).
- **Linear algebra:** `solve` and determinants have exact references computed with gmp rationals
  (every double is exactly representable as a rational). Decompositions are instead checked by
  backward error and orthogonality (see below).

## Tolerances

| Area | Relative tolerance |
|---|---|
| Special functions, densities, distribution functions | 1e-14 (non-central t: 1e-13; `ptukey`: 2e-14) |
| Distributions outside base R (inverse gamma, Kumaraswamy, Pareto, triangular, Laplace, arcsine) | 1e-14 for d/p/q, against extraDistr (arcsine: R's beta(1/2, 1/2) moved to (a, b)) and mpmath |
| Quantile functions | 1e-14, or backward error no larger than R's; discrete quantiles exactly equal or verified by definition |
| Random numbers | discrete values, `sample` and `r2dtable` exactly equal, and with L'Ecuyer-CMRG `runif` and the state after each case (`.Random.seed`); continuous values within 1e-12; the number of uniform draws consumed is checked exactly |
| Descriptive statistics | 1e-14 (skewness and kurtosis near 0, which come from cancellation, measured absolutely) |
| Hypothesis tests | 1e-12 for every numeric field (`cor`: 1e-15, `pAdjust`: exact; roots found by `uniroot` with tolerance 1e-4, such as the normal-approximation interval of `ansariTest`: 1e-4); text fields exactly equal |
| Linear models (`lm`, `confint`, `predict`) | 1e-12 for every numeric field |
| Generalized linear models (`glm`, `glmSummary`, `glmResiduals`, `glmInfluence`, `glmPredict`, `glmAnova`) | 1e-12 for every numeric field (quantities that cancel compared as described below); iteration counts, ranks, `converged`, `boundary`, warnings and column names exactly equal |
| `glmConfint` | 1e-10 against exact profile likelihood limits, relative to the larger of the limit and the standard error |
| Linear algebra | 1e-14 (`crossprod`, `norm`, `backsolve`, `chol`, `qr`, the 2-norm from `svd`, matrix arithmetic, `seq`, `lu`) or 1e-13 (`solve`, `determinant`, `rcond`, `svd`, `kappa`, `ginv`, `eigen`), measured relative to the norm of the result; `prcomp` 1e-12; pivots, ranks and signs of determinants exactly equal |
| Numerical methods | 1e-15 (`approx`), 1e-14 (`integrate`, `optimize`, `uniroot`, `barylag`, `hermiteInterp`, `ode`, and `grad`, `jacobian`, `hessian` with an absolute floor of 1e-12) or 1e-13 (`spline`, `splinefun`, `splinefunH`) |

The tolerances were chosen per area from measurement: the largest observed errors against R or
the reference are just below them.

### Why some tolerances are wider

- **`ptukey` (2e-14):** R's `ptukey` is accurate to about 8 digits, so nearly every case is
  compared with the reference. The reference is a 160-bit trapezoidal rule, used only where
  halving the step changed the value by less than 1e-18. Most errors are below 6e-15; the
  largest is 1.7e-14 at `ptukey(0.5, 1000, 10)` ≈ 1.3e-129, where the rounding of log W
  (ε |log W|) dominates.
- **Non-central t (1e-13):** P[T ≤ t] = E[Φ(tS − δ)] is integrated with the trapezoidal rule.
  In the deep tail (below 1e-50) the exponents at the nodes are in the hundreds, and the error
  against the reference approaches 1e-13 (about 1e-15 near the centre).

### Random numbers

The continuous values cannot match exactly because the last bits of `Math.log`, `Math.exp`,
`Math.pow` and `Math.tan` differ between V8 and glibc's libm. The largest observed difference is
1.2e-13, for gamma variates with very small shape (e.g. 0.001), where exp(log(u)/shape) magnifies
the error by 1/shape. The number of uniform draws is checked by drawing `runif(1)` after each
case: a single different accept/reject decision would show up there.

The other RNG kinds (`rngRounding`, `rngLecuyer`, `rngStreams`) are checked the same way, and also
compare the state afterwards (`getRandomSeed()` against R's `.Random.seed`: the kind code, and all
seven integers for L'Ecuyer-CMRG). L'Ecuyer-CMRG's uniforms are computed with exact integer
arithmetic in doubles, so `runif` and everything discrete match R exactly; continuous variates
(`rnorm`, `rexp`, `rgamma`) are compared within 1e-12 as above.

### Hypothesis tests

- Text fields (`method`, statistic names and so on) must match exactly. Inputs for which R
  throws an error must throw.
- Fields determined by cancellation are measured against a scale floor,
  |difference| / max(|expected|, scale): the t-test interval uses |mu|; the Wilcoxon interval and
  estimate use the largest absolute data value and |mu| (when the true root is 0).
- Fisher's test for tables of 3 × 3 or larger cannot be given a reference in reasonable time, so
  a relative difference up to 1e-10 there is recorded as a known difference.
- The normal-approximation limits and estimate of `ansariTest` are roots found by `uniroot` with
  tolerance 1e-4, in R as here, so they are compared with a tolerance of 1e-4. Since the same root
  finder is evaluated on the same step function, they agree with R to 3e-15. Where R fails with
  "object 'cint' not found" (24 cases, see [differences-from-r.md](differences-from-r.md)), the
  result is compared with the oracle's repetition of the case with `exact = FALSE`.
- Simulated p-values (`chisqTestSim`, `fisherTestSim`) must match exactly: each case calls
  `setSeed(seed)` before the test, and the `runif(1)` drawn afterwards must equal R's, which checks
  the number of uniforms consumed.
- `chisqTest(x, y)` sorts the levels by code point where R used the locale's collation; in the one
  fixture case where the orders differ, R's rows and columns are reordered before comparing.

### Linear algebra

- Matrices and vectors are compared normwise (|difference| / largest absolute value of the
  field), because entries near zero are determined by cancellation and cannot be compared
  elementwise. `crossprod` uses the product of the column norms as the scale.
- **Condition-number scaling.** R uses OpenBLAS, whose operation order differs from rStats.js.
  The rounding differences grow with the condition number κ, which the oracle stores in
  `args.cond`. The allowed error is scale × max(tolerance, 10 ε κ^k), with k from the known
  sensitivity: 1/2 for `chol`; 1 for `solve`, determinants, `rcond`, `kappa`, `qr` and `ginv`;
  2 for least-squares coefficients (`qrCoef`, `qrSolve`), whose error is proportional to κ²
  when the residual is non-zero.
- **Eigenvectors and singular vectors** are compared after aligning signs (or phases, for
  complex vectors). Their error is proportional to ε × largest value / gap between values, so
  the tolerance is widened by the smallest relative gap stored by the oracle (`gap`). Vectors for
  repeated values (relative gap below 1e-8) and for zero singular values are not unique and are
  not compared.
- **`qr` with dependent columns:** Householder vectors for columns after the numerical rank
  (singular values above 1e-13 × the largest) are determined only by rounding and are not
  compared. For rank-deficient matrices in LAPACK mode, Q, `qty`, `qy` and coefficients are not
  compared either.
- **Invariants.** Every decomposition is checked for backward error and orthogonality
  (‖A − QR‖, ‖UDVᵀ − A‖, ‖QᵀQ − I‖ and so on, within 10 × dimension × ε) in
  `test/linalg-invariants.test.ts`.
- Pivots and ranks of `qr` and `chol` match R exactly, including R's limited column pivoting.
- Linear-algebra fixtures record the LAPACK and BLAS versions R used in their `info` block,
  because a different OpenBLAS version can change the last bits.

### Generalized linear models

The fixtures (`oracle/glm.R`, `oracle/glmconfint.R`, `oracle/glmanova.R`) cover every family of R's
stats with every link it accepts (quasi with its variance functions and power links), two-column
binomial responses, weights (with zeros), offsets, no intercept, aliased columns, missing values,
starting values, step halving at the boundary, separation, non-convergence and the inputs R rejects.

- **`glm`** (131 cases, 11 of them errors): with R's default epsilon = 1e-8 the fits stop where R's
  stop, up to 2.5e-4 away from the maximum, so they can only agree if the iterations are R's (see
  [differences-from-r.md](differences-from-r.md#linear-models)). They agree to 1e-12 in every field;
  the largest errors are 2.3e-13 (coefficients), 4.3e-13 (linear predictors), 1.3e-13 (deviance),
  6.0e-13 (p-values), 3.9e-13 (leverages) and 9.5e-13 (residuals). The 37 cases with epsilon =
  1e-14 agree to 6.7e-15 (coefficients). Those are not the maximum likelihood estimate to rounding
  for non-canonical links: there R's rule stops when the deviance stops changing, up to 1e-7 from the
  estimate (cauchit, log-binomial; the score is about 1e-7 there, 1e-15 after iterating further).
  Quantities that are determined by cancellation are compared on a scale that reflects it:
  - deviance residuals, `rstandard` and the standard errors of predictions are square roots of sums
    that cancel near 0 (deviance contributions, variances of predictions at a point of huge weight);
    they are compared as squares, to 1e-12 of the largest square in the field (85 values), and Cook's
    distances, proportional to a squared residual, as square roots (12 values);
  - linear predictors near 0, relative to the size of their terms |x_ij b_j| (3 values);
  - perfect fits (deviance below 1e-20 times the null deviance): the residuals, the deviance and the
    AIC are rounding residue, in R as here (11 values);
  - a fit that R stops at the boundary of the valid range: quantities that depend on a mean within d
    of the end of its range are determined to eps / d (9 values, largest 1.1e-7 at d = 5.8e-9);
  - separation (R warns "fitted probabilities numerically 0 or 1"): the estimates go to infinity and
    R's are where its iterations stop; tolerance 1e-8 (2 cases; largest 3.8e-9, for the weights,
    which depend on exp(−|eta|)).
- **`glmAnova`** (57 cases): 2.1e-13; the deviance of a term is a difference of residual deviances
  (exactly 0 for an aliased term) and a saturated model has a residual deviance of rounding residue,
  so the deviance columns, and the F statistics computed from them, are compared relative to the
  largest residual deviance of the table (4 values).
- **`glmConfint`** (30 cases): R's limits come from a spline through a grid and are up to 4.9e-4
  standard errors away from the exact limits; the fixture holds exact references (`uniroot` on refits
  with epsilon = 1e-14). 20 cases agree with them to 1e-12 (relative to the larger of the limit and
  the standard error). In the other 10, the references' refits stop by R's rule before the quantities
  that are not stationary at the estimate have converged: the Pearson dispersion of the estimated
  families (R computes it with the working weights of the iteration before the last) and the score
  statistic of `test: "Rao"`. There glmConfint, which iterates to rounding, differs from the
  references by 3.1e-10 to 7.1e-8 standard errors, and following R's rule instead reproduces them to
  8.3e-15 (one Rao case to 2.1e-10).

## Known accuracy problems

None are known in the tested ranges: every case matches R or a high-precision reference within
the tolerance.

Outside them, `besselJ` and `besselY` with orders in the thousands and x < ν have relative errors
of about 1.0–1.5e-14 (for example 1.04e-14 for `besselY(3000, 3949)` and 9.9e-15 for
`besselJ(1000, 1500)`), from rounding that accumulates over the many steps of the recurrence and
the continued fractions.

R's `qnbinom` does not terminate for 13 inputs (e.g. `qnbinom(-1, 0.5, 1e-10, log.p = TRUE)`).
`oracle/nbinom.R` limits each case to 2 seconds and drops those rows from the fixture.

## Where the accuracy comes from

Most kernels are ported from SciPy's xsf (BSD-3) and Boost.Math (BSL-1.0); linear algebra follows
the LAPACK reference implementation (modified BSD), LINPACK and EISPACK (public domain). See
[THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). The main changes made to match or exceed R:

- **Exponents in double-double.** A probability computed as exp(−E) has a relative error equal
  to the absolute error of E. When E is in the hundreds (probabilities near 1e-300), double
  precision alone reaches 1e-13. Loader's saddle-point terms (`src/special/loader.ts`) and the
  arguments of exp(−t) in the exponential, Weibull, logistic and log-normal distributions
  (`src/internal/ddexp.ts`) are formed in double-double; at t = 700 the rounding of t alone would
  cost 6e-14.
- **x and 1 − x as an exact pair.** Multiplying a rounded 1 − x by a large exponent (b = 1e5)
  magnifies its rounding error. The incomplete beta function works from the smaller of the two
  and forms the other in double-double; the t and F distributions pass n/(n + t²) and similar
  quantities in double-double.
- **Continued fractions in double-double** (`src/special/incbet.ts`): with large parameters and
  x near the mean, the continued fraction is extremely sensitive to the rounding of x (6e-13 at
  a = 1e5, x = 0.999). The coefficients a + b and b − 1 are double-double too, since rounding
  a + b loses the digits of a small b (5e-11 at a = 1e8, b = 0.01), and from 2^52 on all of them
  are, since steps of 1 would be lost.
- **Uniform asymptotic expansion for large parameters** (`src/special/incbetTemme.ts`): with
  ab/(a + b) >= 25 and x within the region where the expansion converges, the incomplete beta
  function uses Temme's expansion, whose exponent is the same double-double deviance as Loader's
  binomial density; in the tails a rearranged form avoids the cancellation between the erfc term
  and the correction (see [performance.md](performance.md)).
- **Asymptotic expansion for one large and one small parameter** (`src/special/incbetLargeA.ts`):
  with a >= 15, b <= 40 and x in the bulk, DiDonato and Morris's expansion in incomplete gamma
  functions of u = −(a + (b − 1)/2) log x replaces the continued fraction. Its prefactor comes from
  the same double-double deviance as Loader's binomial density, u is formed in double-double, and
  the leading incomplete gamma function comes from a continued fraction evaluated backwards, finished
  in double-double (the forward evaluation in `igam.ts` stops too early for small u), so the cases
  that take it are within 1.2e-15 (see [performance.md](performance.md)).
- **Both tails computed directly**, never as 1 − (other tail). The complement of the series,
  1 − I_x(a, b), uses a subtraction-free formula.
- **lgamma(b + a) − lgamma(b) without subtraction** (`src/special/lgammaDiff.ts`) for a ≪ b, used
  by `lbeta`, prefactors and series complements. Near the zeros of lgamma (1 and 2) it uses
  lgamma1p (xsf's formula has relative error 6e-7 at x = 1 + 1e-10).
- **Quantile solver** (`src/internal/invert.ts`): Newton/Halley iteration in u = log x, finished
  in x itself when |log x| is large (the step in u then limits the precision of x). A step that
  does not move because of rounding counts as convergence. `qlnorm` applies one double-double
  Newton correction to the normal quantile, because the rounding of z is magnified by
  sdlog |z|. `qgeom` uses a closed form. Discrete binary searches stop when the answer exceeds
  2^53.
- **`pnorm` tails** split q = hi + lo (hi on a 1/16 grid) to compute exp(−q²/2) accurately.
  `qnorm` with `logP` below −2048 (beyond Cephes' tail approximation) adds one Newton step using
  `pnorm`.
- **Wilcoxon rank sum** uses Harding's (1984) q-binomial product in double-double; in double
  precision it is off by 1e-13 at m = n = 150.
- **Studentized range** uses Copenhaver and Holland's double integral with adaptive
  Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature, the outer integral in v = log(t/a), and the upper tail integrated
  directly.
- **Non-central distributions** (`src/internal/poissonMixture.ts`): the Poisson mixture is summed
  from the mode outwards with positive terms only, recurrences in double-double, and the exponent
  kept separately (mantissa × 2^(500k)), so log answers stay accurate below 1e-300. The
  non-central t integrates a positive integrand after a one-sided double-exponential change of
  variables, so opposite signs of t and δ cause no cancellation; far tails use an integrated-by-
  parts form and then the |t|^(−ν) power law. The non-central chi-squared density uses the
  asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function for very large x × ncp.
- **Sums for tests** (`src/internal/summary.ts`): means, variances and correlations are summed in
  double-double, and deviations are rescaled by powers of two before multiplying, so data around
  1e±200 neither overflow nor underflow.

The cost of this in speed, and how to measure what double-double buys, is described in
[performance.md](performance.md).
